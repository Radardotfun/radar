Privacy policy · Updated October 29, 2026

Privacy

Radar has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. A post only leaves your device when you ask the radar to read it.

What it reads

When you tap the radar on a post on X (or search from the toolbar button), the extension reads that post: its text, the author's handle and name, the handles it replies to, the post it replies to or quotes, link previews, and its images or video. It does not read anything else on the page, your messages, your account or your browsing history.

Posts on screen that contain a token contract address also have that address looked up on DexScreener, to show the coin's ticker, market cap and age under the post. Only the address is sent, not the post.

Where it goes

  • Our server (narrative-radar.millenia-portfolio.workers.dev, on Cloudflare) receives that post to work out its narrative. It passes the post to an AI model to do this: Anthropic's Claude, or as a backup Groq, Cerebras, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Mistral or Cloudflare Workers AI. Videos are transcribed by Cloudflare Workers AI.
  • Larp check: for the post you scan, our server also reads the public X profile of the author and of the coin's linked account (through FxTwitter), looks up the registration date of the coin's website and of links in the post (RDAP), and opens those links to see where they lead. Nothing about you is included.
  • Market data services receive coin names, tickers and contract addresses, never your post: DexScreener (prices and pools), RugCheck (token audits), Larpcheck (larpcheck.app, its published verdicts on a coin, looked up by our server with the contract address only) and the Solana network (launch and holder data).

Results for a post are cached on our server for a few minutes (video transcripts for up to 24 hours) so a repeat scan is instant. Your IP address is used only to limit how many scans one person can run. Cloudflare, which hosts the server, keeps standard request logs for a few days. Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared for advertising.

What it stores on your device

Whether you've seen the first-run tip, and a cache of on-chain launch data so bundle checks are faster next time, in your browser's extension storage. If you connect Frontrun, your Frontrun API key is kept there too, on this device only, and is sent only to solana.frontrun.pro. Your last 8 scans that found a coin (the post's link or the words you searched, the narrative and the coin) are kept there as well, so the panel can list them under Recent, and Clear removes them. Removing the extension deletes all of it.

Alerts

When you tap the bell on a post, the extension keeps that post (its text, author, link and the narrative read from it) in your browser's extension storage for 24 hours, along with the coins it has already seen and the alerts it has shown. Once a minute it searches DexScreener for the post's terms and reads DexScreener's newest coin profiles. When a new coin turns up, it runs the same larp check and holder check as a scan. Alerts show on your open X tabs, on the toolbar icon and, only if you turn them on, as desktop notifications. Nothing about your watches leaves your device except those searches and checks.

Quick Buy

Insta Buy opens tryradar.fun/trade in a small window with the coin's contract address and the amount you picked; the full buy page opens it in a tab. When the buy starts, the page sends that address, your wallet's public address, the amount and your slippage to PumpPortal, which builds the transaction. Radar simulates it and passes it to your wallet, which shows it and signs it only if you approve. The signed buy goes to Solana through Radar's RPC relay. Radar never asks for your seed phrase or private key, never holds your SOL, and does not store your wallet address. The page remembers your slippage and which wallet app you used (its name, not its address) in your browser only. PumpPortal charges 0.5% per buy; see its fees and terms.

If you launch a coin on tryradar.fun/launch, your wallet connects there and signs the launch; Radar never sees its key. Radar's server stores the coin's name, ticker, description and picture, the post's link and your wallet address. The picture and text are public and stay online for the coin, and the launch itself is public on Solana for good. tryradar.fun/launched lists the coin, without your wallet address.

On Robinhood Chain, Insta Buy opens tryradar.fun/trade-robinhood the same way. The page reads the coin's pools from DexScreener and simulates the buy and a sale on Robinhood Chain's public node, with your wallet's public address once it's connected. Your wallet shows the buy, signs it and sends it. Radar takes no fee, never holds your ETH and does not store your wallet address.

If you connect Frontrun instead, buys go from your Frontrun wallet: Radar sends the order to Frontrun with your key, and Frontrun holds that wallet and signs. Frontrun's own terms and fees apply.

Holder check

If you choose to check your wallet for $RADAR, your wallet signs a short message on tryradar.fun/holder. That signature can't move funds. Radar's server checks it and reads your balance on-chain, then gives your browser a token that lasts a day and contains your wallet's public address. Radar doesn't keep a list of wallets. While you're checked, the X account of the post you're scanning is sent to Radar's server, which looks it up in Radar's own record (below) and, if it's turned on, with Frontrun.

Radar's record of public posts

When a post that writes out a contract address is scanned, Radar's server reads that public post again and keeps four things: the X account, the post's ID, the contract address and when it was posted. Later it checks whether the post is still up. $RADAR holders can see this record in the panel. It holds nothing about who scanned the post.

Radar on the web

On tryradar.fun/scan, the post link you paste goes to our server, which reads that public post through FxTwitter (api.fxtwitter.com) and works out its narrative the same way as for the extension. Everything else runs in your browser and goes to the same services listed above. The page keeps its cache of on-chain launch data, and the trading terminal you last picked, in your browser's local storage. The address of a post you scan is added to the page's own address, so you can share the scan. Your last 8 scans that found a coin (the post's link or the words you searched, the narrative and the coin) stay in your browser's local storage for the Recent list, and Clear removes them; they aren't sent anywhere. When you open a scan from a reply by the Radar bot, the page adds one to a daily total of those visits. That total holds no address, post or anything else about you.

Links it opens

Buttons for Axiom, Padre, Fomo, GMGN, DexScreener, Solscan, RugCheck, Larpcheck and Bubblemaps open those sites with the coin's address. What happens there is governed by their own policies.
